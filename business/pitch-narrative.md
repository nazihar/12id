# 12iD: the identity wallet with biometric verification

## 12iD in one sentence

12iD puts proof of who a person is, and what they are entitled to, into a secure wallet on their own phone, and confirms with a face check that the right person is holding it.

## 12iD in 30 seconds

Today, proving something about yourself means handing over documents. Organisations copy them, store them, and check them by hand. It is slow, it is easy to cheat, and every stored copy is a risk.

With 12iD, an organisation gives a person a digital credential: a verified customer status, a licence, a permit, a membership. The person keeps it on their phone. When someone needs proof, the phone shows only the fact that was asked for, and the answer can be trusted on the spot. Where it matters, the person also looks at the camera, and 12iD confirms that a real, living person with the right face is present.

Organisations get proof they can rely on. People keep control of their own data. No one has to store piles of identity documents.

## The everyday picture

Think of a physical wallet. It holds cards issued by others: a driving licence, a staff badge, a health card. You carry them. You decide when to show them. The person checking looks at the card, and then looks at your face.

12iD is that wallet and that look at your face, in digital form. It also fixes three weaknesses of the physical version:

- **A plastic card can be forged.** A 12iD credential is signed by the organisation that issued it and cannot be altered.
- **A card shows everything printed on it.** A 12iD credential shows only what was asked.
- **A card can be borrowed.** A 12iD credential cannot be copied to another phone, and the face check confirms who is holding it.

## The problems today

### For organisations

- **The same person is checked again and again.** Every new service, branch or partner repeats the same identity check, at the same cost.
- **Documents are easy to fake.** Scans, photos and paper certificates can be edited. Staff cannot tell a good forgery from the real thing.
- **Passwords and codes prove nothing about the person.** They can be shared, stolen or phished. The system knows a password was typed, not who typed it.
- **Stored copies are a liability.** Every copied ID sits in a database waiting for a breach, a fine and a headline.
- **Checking takes days.** To confirm a certificate or a permit, someone has to find the original record or call head office.

### For people

- They hand over a whole document to prove a single fact.
- They fill in the same forms for every organisation.
- They have no idea where their copies end up, or for how long.

### For governments and regulators

- Large central databases of citizens' data are attractive targets.
- Paper licences and permits are forged, and checking them at a counter or in the field is slow.
- Privacy rules ask for less data to be collected and kept, while services are expected to become faster and more digital.

## What 12iD is: one complete solution

12iD covers the whole journey, from giving a credential to checking it.

## How it works, in three steps

The steps are always the same: issue, hold, prove. Here they are for a bank and for a government agency.

### A bank and its customer

**1. Issue.** A new customer opens an account in the bank's app. The bank checks their identity documents once, as it does today. The customer then looks at the phone camera, and 12iD confirms that a real person is present and that their face matches the photo the bank holds from that check. The bank places a "verified customer" credential in the wallet inside its app.

**2. Hold.** The credential stays on the customer's phone, protected by the phone's secure hardware. 12iD keeps no copy of it. It carries an expiry date, and the bank can revoke it at any time.

**3. Prove.** Weeks later, the customer sends a large transfer, adds a new payee or applies for a loan. The amount and the payee appear in the app, and the bank asks the wallet for proof instead of sending a code by text message. The customer sees exactly what they are approving and agrees. For a high-value action the bank also requires the face check, so it knows the verified customer in person is approving it, not someone holding their phone.

No documents uploaded a second time. No code for a fraudster to steal. No visit to a branch.

### A government agency and a citizen

**1. Issue.** A citizen applies for a licence, a permit or a benefit. The agency approves it and sends a digital credential to the wallet in the agency's app. Before it arrives, the citizen passes the face check against the photo the agency holds on file.

**2. Hold.** The credential stays on the citizen's phone, under their control, until it expires or the agency revokes it.

**3. Prove.** At a service counter, on the agency's website or during a field inspection, the officer asks the wallet for proof. The citizen agrees, and the officer gets a confirmed answer on the spot: the permit is valid, and the person in front of them is its holder. The officer sees that fact and nothing else from the citizen's file.

Nobody searched a register. Nobody photocopied a document. Nobody saw more than they needed.

## What makes 12iD different

**Prove a fact without showing the data.** A person can prove "I am over 18" without showing a birth date, "my identity has been verified" without showing the documents again, or "my permit is valid" without showing its details. The organisation gets the answer and none of the surplus.

**No lookup, no phone call.** The proof carries its own evidence. Checking it does not depend on someone finding the original record or reaching the office that issued it.

**Tied to a living person, not just a device.** A credential on its own proves that a phone holds it. The biometric face check proves who is holding the phone. A printed photo or a face on a screen is not a living person, and the check is designed to tell the difference.

**No central vault of credentials.** Credentials live on people's phones, not in one large database. There is no single place to break into and take everyone's identity.

**Your app, your brand.** People do not install an unfamiliar third-party app. The wallet is part of the app they already use with you.

## Privacy and security in plain words

- **The credential is on the phone only.** 12iD keeps no copy and cannot read the wallet.
- **Messages are sealed end to end.** What travels between the phone and your organisation cannot be read on the way.
- **Face images are not kept.** The photo used for a check is held, encrypted, only while the check runs, and is deleted once the result is known. Only the result stays.
- **Records do not live forever.** Personal details of finished exchanges are erased automatically after a period you set. You can also erase one person's data on request, immediately.
- **Everything is traceable.** Changes and sensitive actions in the console are written to an audit log.
- **Strong sign-in for your team.** Console access is by invitation, with two-step sign-in for everyone.
- **Protected and recoverable.** Each organisation's records are kept apart from every other's. Backups are made every night, encrypted, and copied to a second site.

12iD is designed to support data protection obligations such as data minimisation and the right to erasure. Your organisation stays in control of its users' data; 12iD processes it only to run the service.

## The technology underneath

For the technical people in the room, in one line each:

- **Verifiable credentials.** Digitally signed statements that anyone can check and nobody can alter.
- **Zero-knowledge proofs (AnonCreds).** The mathematics that lets a person prove a fact without revealing the value behind it.
- **A distributed ledger (Hyperledger Indy).** A shared public record of which organisations issue which credentials. It holds no personal data.
- **End-to-end encrypted messaging (DIDComm).** A private channel between the wallet and the organisation.
- **Secure hardware on the phone.** The wallet's keys are locked to the device and never leave it.
- **Face liveness and face matching.** 12iD's biometric check that a real person is present and is the right person.
- **Open standards.** The credentials are built on published, open technology, not a closed format.

## Questions people ask

**Is our data on a blockchain?**
No. The ledger holds only public information about issuing organisations, so that signatures can be checked. No personal data, and no credential, is ever written to it.

**What if someone loses their phone?**
The credentials on it cannot be used by anyone else, because they are locked to that phone. The person gets a new wallet and you issue their credentials again.

**What if a phone is stolen and unlocked?**
This is why the face check exists. Where you require it, the person holding the phone must pass it, and a thief will not.

**Do you keep people's faces?**
No. The photo is used for the check and deleted when the check is decided. What remains is the result.

**Do people need to install another app?**
No. The wallet is part of your organisation's own app.

**How does a credential stop being valid?**
Every credential has an expiry date, and every check confirms it automatically. If a credential must stop sooner, for example when an account is closed or a permit is withdrawn, you revoke it and it is refused from then on. When the facts change, you issue a new credential.

**Who can see what our users share?**
Your organisation sees what your users agree to share with you. 12iD support staff see that information masked, and must record a reason to view it.
