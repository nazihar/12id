# 12iD pitch narrative: the source for every pitch

## Part 1: How to use this document

This document is the source for every 12iD pitch. It is not sent to a client as it is. A pitch is built by picking blocks from it and joining them:

**A pitch = the core story (Part 2) + the blocks for its readers (Part 3) + one industry block (Part 5) + the matching questions (Part 6) + the closing blocks (Part 7).**

| Part | What it holds | Goes into |
|---|---|---|
| 2. Core story | What 12iD is, how it works, why it is different | Every pitch |
| 3. By reader | Four readers found in most pitches (management; product and digital channels; technical; procurement, legal and compliance) and five more for specific cases (risk and fraud; finance; operations; the people who will use it; implementation partners) | The blocks that match who will read the pitch |
| 4. By use case | Eight use cases that repeat in every industry | The use case a pitch leads with; the base for an industry that has no block |
| 5. By industry | Eight industries, each told in the same six parts, with banks, government and healthcare first; eight more in brief | One block per pitch |
| 6. Questions and answers | Grouped by reader, plus how 12iD compares with what clients use today | The groups that match |
| 7. Closing blocks | Evidence, who 12iD is, the next step | Every pitch |
| 8. Wording and recipes | Terms, style, taglines, which blocks make which document, market notes, open fields, plain-word definitions | Followed, never copied |

Three rules:

- **Copy the block text; do not rewrite it.** It is written for the client. Make a pitch shorter by leaving blocks out, not by rewording them.
- **Never copy a writer's note.** Lines in italics that start with *Writer's note* are for the person assembling the pitch.
- **Fill every `[to fill …]` in the pitch, not here.** These fields stay open in this document, because their answers change with the client, the market and the date. They are listed in 8.6.

## Part 2: Core story

*Writer's note: the title of a pitch is "12iD: the digital identity wallet with a biometric face check".*

### 2.1 The opening

*Writer's note: use one length only. One sentence for a cover or an email. One paragraph for a summary page. Thirty seconds for the first page of a full pitch.*

**One sentence.**
12iD puts proof of who a person is, and what they are entitled to, into a secure wallet on their own mobile device, and confirms with a biometric face check that the right person is holding it.

**One paragraph.**
Organisations spend time and money checking the same documents again and again, and still get forgeries, impersonation and data breaches. 12iD replaces the paperwork with digital credentials that people carry in a secure wallet on their own mobile device. A person proves just the fact that is needed, the organisation gets an answer it can trust on the spot, and a biometric face check confirms that the real holder is present. Less fraud, less stored data, faster service, and people stay in control of their identity.

**Thirty seconds.**
Today, proving something about yourself means handing over documents. Organisations copy them, store them, and check them by hand. It is slow, it is open to fraud, and every stored copy is a risk.

With 12iD, an organisation gives a person a digital credential: a verified customer status, a licence, a permit, a membership. The person keeps it on their mobile device. When someone needs proof, the device shows only the fact that was asked for, and the answer can be trusted on the spot. Where higher assurance is needed, the person also passes a biometric face check: they look at the camera, and 12iD confirms that a real, living person with the right face is present.

Organisations get proof they can rely on. Individuals keep control of their own data. No one has to collect the same documents again.

### 2.2 The problems today

*Writer's note: for a general pitch. In an industry pitch, the industry block's own problem list replaces "For organisations".*

**For organisations**

- **The same person is checked again and again.** Every new service, branch or partner repeats the same identity check, at the same cost.
- **Documents are easy to forge.** Scans, photos and paper certificates can be edited. Staff cannot reliably tell a good forgery from a genuine document.
- **Passwords and one-time codes prove nothing about the person.** They can be shared, stolen or phished. The system knows a password was typed, not who typed it.
- **Stored copies are a liability.** Every copied ID sits in a database waiting for a breach, a fine and a headline.
- **Checking takes days.** To confirm a certificate or a permit, someone has to find the original record or contact the issuing office.

**For individuals**

- They hand over a whole document to prove a single fact.
- They fill in the same forms for every organisation.
- They cannot see where their copies are kept, who can access them, or for how long.

### 2.3 The everyday picture

Think of a physical wallet. It holds cards issued by others: a driving licence, a staff badge, a health card. You carry them. You decide when to show them. The person checking looks at the card, and then looks at your face.

12iD is that wallet and that look at your face, in digital form. It also fixes three weaknesses of the physical version:

- **A plastic card can be forged.** A 12iD credential is signed by the organisation that issued it and cannot be altered.
- **A card shows everything printed on it.** A 12iD credential shows only what was asked.
- **A card can be borrowed.** A 12iD credential cannot be copied to another device, and the face check confirms who is holding it.

### 2.4 What 12iD is: one complete solution

12iD covers the full credential lifecycle, from issuing a credential to verifying it.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Issue** | You turn a fact you know about a person into a signed digital credential. |
| **Hold** | The person keeps the credential in a secure wallet on their mobile device. The wallet lives inside your own mobile app, under your own brand. |
| **Verify** | Whenever you need proof, at a counter, online or in the field, you ask the wallet and get an answer you can trust, without looking up a record. |
| **Biometric face check** | 12iD confirms that a live person is present and that their face matches the photo you hold on record. |
| **Manage** | A web console where your team defines credentials, sets the rules, and reviews the full audit history of what was issued and verified. |

These are not separate products to assemble. They are designed, delivered and supported together.

### 2.5 How it works, in three steps

*Writer's note: this is the version without an industry, for a general pitch. In an industry pitch, leave it out: the scenario in the industry block tells the same three steps as a story.*

The steps are always the same: issue, hold, prove.

**1. Issue.** You confirm a fact about a person, as you do today: their identity, a licence, a membership. Where you require it, the person passes the face check against the photo you hold on record. You then place a signed credential in the wallet inside your app.

**2. Hold.** The credential stays on the person's mobile device, protected by the device's secure hardware. 12iD keeps no copy of it. It carries an expiry date, and you can revoke it at any time.

**3. Prove.** When you need proof, you ask the wallet. The person sees what is asked and agrees. You get an answer you can trust on the spot, and only the fact you asked for. For a sensitive action you also require the face check, so you know the right person is holding the device.

### 2.6 What makes 12iD different

**Prove a fact without showing the data.** A person can prove "I am over 18" without showing a birth date, "my identity has been verified" without showing the documents again, or "my permit is valid" without showing its details. You get the answer and nothing more.

**No lookup, no phone call.** The proof carries its own evidence. Checking it does not depend on someone finding the original record or reaching the office that issued it.

**Tied to a living person, not just a device.** A credential on its own proves that a device holds it. The face check proves who is holding the device. A printed photo or a face on a screen is not a living person, and the check is designed to tell the difference.

**No central vault of credentials.** Credentials live on people's own devices, not in one large database. There is no single place to break into and take everyone's identity.

**Your app, your brand.** People do not install an unfamiliar third-party app. The wallet is part of the app they already use with you.

### 3.1 Management

*Writer's note: this reader cares about cost, fraud losses, customer experience, reputation and regulatory exposure. Talk about outcomes. Use no technology names. Questions: 6.1.*

**What you gain**

- **Lower verification cost.** Verify once, then reuse the proof instead of repeating the process.
- **Less fraud.** Forged documents and borrowed identities are refused.
- **Less data to protect.** You ask for the fact you need, so you hold less personal data and carry less risk.
- **Faster service.** Onboarding and approvals that took days happen while the customer waits.
- **A full audit record.** Every credential issued and every check made is in the console, with who did what and when.

**What the people you serve gain**

- Their credentials are on their own device, under their control.
- They see what is asked and decide whether to share it.

### 3.2 Product and digital channels

*Writer's note: this reader owns the app or the service channel: the head of digital banking, of e-services, of patient services. They care about how many people finish a journey, how long it takes, and what the person sees. They are often the one who carries 12iD forward inside the organisation. Questions: 6.2.*

**What changes in your app**

- **Fewer steps.** An approval happens inside your app. The person sees the request, agrees, and where you require it looks at the camera. There is no code to wait for and type in, and no document to photograph again.
- **Your design.** A request for approval appears in your app's own design and wording. The person never leaves your app.
- **You decide where the face check applies.** You set it for each credential and each kind of request. For a request you can also set it by rule, using numbers your own systems already know: an amount, a risk score, whether the device is new, the days since the last check. Routine actions stay quick, and only the risky ones add a step.
- **A clear path when something goes wrong.** A person with a new device gets their credentials issued again. A person who does not pass the face check has a set number of attempts, and then your own process takes over.

### 3.3 Technical: IT, security and architecture

*Writer's note: this reader cares about how 12iD connects to their systems, what is stored where, how it is protected, and which standards it uses. This is the only block where technology names appear. Questions: 6.3.*

**How it connects to your systems**

- **In your mobile app.** The 12iD SDK adds the wallet to the app you already have. Your app keeps its own screens and its own brand.
- **In your backend.** Your systems call the 12iD API to issue a credential or to ask for proof, and receive each result as a signed notification.
- **For your team.** The web console is where credentials and rules are defined and the audit history is reviewed.

**Privacy and security in plain terms**

- **The credential is on the device only.** 12iD keeps no copy and cannot read the wallet.
- **Messages are encrypted end to end.** What travels between the device and your organisation cannot be read in transit.
- **Face images are not kept.** The photo used for a check is held, encrypted, only while the check runs, and is deleted once the result is known. Only the result stays.
- **Records are not kept indefinitely.** Personal details of finished exchanges are erased automatically after a period you set. You can also erase one person's data on request, immediately.
- **Everything is traceable.** Changes and sensitive actions in the console are written to an audit log.
- **Strong sign-in for your team.** Console access is by invitation, with two-factor authentication for every team member.
- **Protected and recoverable.** Each organisation's records are kept separate from every other organisation's. Backups are made every night, encrypted, and copied to a second site.
- **The face check cannot be skipped.** 12iD enforces it, not the app. A credential is not issued, and a result is not released to you, until the check has passed, whatever the app on the device does.

**The technology foundation**

- **Verifiable credentials.** Digitally signed statements that anyone can check and nobody can alter.
- **Zero-knowledge proofs (AnonCreds).** The cryptography that lets a person prove a fact without revealing the value behind it.
- **A distributed ledger (Hyperledger Indy).** A shared public record of which organisations issue which credentials. It holds no personal data.
- **End-to-end encrypted messaging (DIDComm).** A private channel between the wallet and the organisation.
- **Secure hardware on the device.** The wallet's keys are locked to the device and never leave it.
- **Face liveness and face matching.** The two halves of 12iD's face check: a real person is present, and it is the right person.
- **Open standards.** The credentials are built on published, open technology, not a closed format.

### 3.5 Risk, fraud and security operations

*Writer's note: this reader counts losses and investigates cases: the head of fraud, the head of risk, the information security officer. They care about which attacks stop working, what is left, and what evidence they have afterwards. Be exact, and never promise that fraud ends. Questions: 6.6.*

**Which attacks stop working**

| The attack today | With 12iD |
|---|---|
| A forged or edited document | A credential is signed by the organisation that issued it and cannot be altered. |
| A stolen password, or a one-time code read out to a caller | There is no code to steal. The approval happens in your app, from the wallet. |
| A registered number taken over by someone else | Nothing is sent to the number. The credential is on the device, not on the number. |
| A credential copied to a second device | A 12iD credential cannot be copied to another device. |
| A borrowed or stolen device | Where you require it, the face check confirms who is holding the device. |
| A printed photo, or a face on a screen, held up to the camera | The check is designed to tell a living person from an image. |

**How the face check is controlled**

- **A rule driven by your own risk signals.** For a request, the face check can depend on up to five conditions, on any numbers your systems send with it: an amount, a score from your fraud engine, whether the device is new, the days since the last check. You choose whether one condition is enough or all of them must hold.
- **A rule you can tighten.** You change it yourself, in the console, after an incident: a lower amount, a lower score, or more kinds of action.
- **A check the app cannot skip.** 12iD enforces the face check itself. A credential is not issued, and a result is not released to you, until the check has passed, whatever the app on the device does.

**What you have afterwards**

- **A record of every check.** Every credential issued and every check made is in the console, with who did what and when.
- **The result of every face check.** The image is deleted; the result stays with the record.

## Part 4: By use case

*Writer's note: these eight use cases repeat in every industry. Use them in two ways. First, to choose what a pitch leads with: lead with one use case, the one that costs the client most today, and mention at most two more. Second, to write for an industry that has no block in Part 5: pick the use cases that apply and tell them with the template in 5.10.*

### 4.1 Enrolment: check once, then reuse

**Today.** Each service checks the same person from the start: documents, forms and a wait.

**With 12iD.** You check the person once, as you do today, and place the result in their wallet as a credential. Every later service asks the wallet, instead of asking the person to start again.

**Examples.** A bank opens an account, and later offers a loan with no second identity check. An agency registers a citizen once for all its online services. A hospital registers a patient once for every later visit.

### 4.2 Approving a sensitive action

**Today.** A password or a one-time code approves the action. Either can be stolen, and neither shows who used it.

**With 12iD.** The person sees the action in your app and approves it from the wallet. Where the risk is higher, you also require the face check. You set the rule, using numbers your own systems already know: for example, every transfer above an amount you choose, or every request your fraud engine scores as risky.

**Examples.** A bank customer approves a large transfer, or a change of their registered mobile number. A citizen confirms new bank details for a benefit payment. A patient approves the release of their test results.

### 4.3 Confirming who is asking: at a counter, on a call, online

**Today.** At a counter, a document is inspected by eye. On a call, the person answers security questions that others can find out. Online, a password is enough.

**With 12iD.** Your staff ask the wallet for proof. The person agrees on their own device, and staff get a confirmed answer on the spot, with the face check where you require it. The step is the same wherever the person is.

**Examples.** A bank's call centre confirms a caller without security questions. An officer checks a permit during a field inspection. A hospital reception confirms a patient's entitlement.

### 4.4 Proving one fact and nothing more

**Today.** To prove one fact, a person hands over a whole document, and the organisation keeps a copy of all of it.

**With 12iD.** You ask one question, and the wallet answers only that: "over 18", "identity verified", "permit valid", "entitled to this service". You never receive the rest.

**Examples.** A bank confirms that a customer is already verified before opening a second product. An agency confirms that a person holds a valid permit without opening their file. A hospital pharmacy confirms a prescription entitlement without seeing the diagnosis.

### 4.5 Getting back in: a new device or a locked account

**Today.** Recovery is the weakest point. It relies on a code sent to a number that may have been taken over, or on a visit in person.

**With 12iD.** The credentials on the old device cannot be used by anyone else. On the new device, the person passes the face check against the photo you hold on record, and you issue their credentials again.

**Examples.** A bank customer changes device and is back in the app without a branch visit. A citizen replaces a lost device and receives their licence again. A patient gets their entitlement back the same day.

### 4.6 Staff, agents and professionals

**Today.** Staff carry badges that can be lent, share passwords, and prove their qualifications on paper.

**With 12iD.** The same wallet serves your own people. A credential carries a person's role, training or clearance. They prove it at the door or online, with the face check for the most sensitive access. It expires with the role or the contract, and you can revoke it at any time.

**Examples.** A bank's tellers and agents prove who they are before they open the systems they work in. An agency's officers sign in to case systems. A clinician proves their registration and their right to work on a ward.

### 4.7 Acting for someone else

**Today.** A parent, a carer or an authorised representative proves their authority with a letter or a stamped form, checked by eye each time.

**With 12iD.** You issue a credential for the authority itself: who may act, for whom, and until when. The representative proves it from their own wallet, with the face check where you require it. It expires on the date you set, and you can revoke it at any time.

**Examples.** A company's authorised signatory approves a payment from a business account. A guardian collects a benefit for a dependant. A carer collects a prescription for a patient.

### 4.8 Confirming that a person is still entitled

**Today.** Entitlement is checked once and then assumed. To check it again, the person is asked to attend in person or to send documents.

**With 12iD.** Every credential has an expiry date, so entitlement is confirmed again each time it is renewed. Where you need to know that the person themselves is still there, they pass the face check from wherever they are.

**Examples.** A bank reviews a long-standing customer's details without a branch visit. A pensioner confirms their entitlement each year from home. An insured member renews their cover.

### 5.2 Government and public services

*Writer's note: for ministries, licensing authorities, municipalities, benefit agencies and regulators.*

**The problem today**

- **Central databases are targets.** Large central databases of citizens' data are attractive targets.
- **Paper is forged and slow to check.** Paper licences and permits are forged, and checking them at a counter or in the field is slow.

## Part 6: Questions and answers

*Writer's note: take the groups that match the reader blocks in the pitch, and add 6.5 whenever the client already uses another method. In a general pitch, use 6.1 to 6.4. Groups 6.6 and 6.7 go with blocks 3.5 and 3.8.*

### 6.1 Management

**Does 12iD replace our identity checks?**
No. You still check a person's identity once, as your rules require. 12iD makes the result of that check reusable, and ties every later action to the person you checked.

**What if a device is stolen and unlocked?**
This is why the face check exists. Where you require it, the person holding the device must pass it, and a thief will not.

**Do we have to change everything at once?**
No. Start with one credential and one use case, for a limited group of people. Your present process stays in place beside it.

### 6.2 Product and digital channels

**Do people need to install another app?**
No. The wallet is part of your organisation's own app.

**Does it work on both Android and iPhone?**
Yes. The wallet is delivered for both.

**What if someone loses their device?**
The credentials on it cannot be used by anyone else, because they are locked to that device. The person gets a new wallet and you issue their credentials again.

**What if a person has no smartphone, or no signal?**
The wallet needs a smartphone, and a data connection at the moment of proof. For people without one, your present process stays in place. 12iD is added to the ways you serve people; it does not close the others.

**What if the face check refuses the right person?**
The person can try again. You set the number of attempts and how strict the check is. After the last attempt the request is refused, and your own process takes over.

**What about people who cannot use the face check?**
You decide where the face check is required. Where a person cannot use it, you offer your present process in its place.

### 6.3 Technical

**Is our data on a blockchain?**
No. The ledger holds only public information about issuing organisations, so that signatures can be checked. No personal data, and no credential, is ever written to it.

**How does a credential stop being valid?**
Every credential has an expiry date, and every check confirms it automatically. If a credential must end sooner, for example when an account is closed or a permit is withdrawn, you revoke it and it is refused from then on. When the facts change, you issue a new credential.

**Do you keep images of people's faces?**
No. The photo is used for the check and deleted once the check is complete. What remains is the result.

**How much work is the integration?**
Three pieces of work on your side: add the wallet to your mobile app, connect your backend to 12iD, and define your credentials in the console. 12iD provides a working example app and backend to start from. `[to fill per client: expected duration]`

**What if we stop using 12iD?**
The credentials are built on published, open technology, not a closed format. You keep your own records of what you issued, and you can run the platform on your own servers.

### 6.4 Procurement, legal and compliance

**Who can see what our users share?**
Your organisation sees what your users agree to share with you. 12iD support staff see that information masked, and must record a reason to view it.

**Where is our data hosted?**
In the country agreed with you. The country where the system and its backups are hosted is written into the agreement.

**Can we erase a person's data?**
Yes. You can erase one person's data on request, immediately, and every erasure is recorded. Personal details of finished exchanges are also erased automatically after a period you set.

### 6.5 How 12iD compares with what you use today

*Writer's note: use the comparisons that match what the client does now. Never name another company. Each answer first says what the other method does well, and then what it leaves open.*

**We already send one-time codes by text message.**
A code shows that someone had the device, or the number, at that moment. It can be read out to a fraudster, intercepted, or sent to a number that has been taken over. With 12iD there is no code to steal: the approval happens in your app, and where you require it the face check confirms the person.

**We already do a selfie check when a customer signs up.**
That check confirms the person on the first day. It says nothing about who is using the account a month later. 12iD turns the result of your sign-up check into a credential the person keeps, and can repeat the face check at any later action you choose.

**Our app already uses the device's fingerprint or face unlock.**
Device unlock compares the person with whoever was set up on that device. Anyone who knows the device's passcode can add their own fingerprint or face. The 12iD face check compares the person with the photo you hold on record, and confirms that a living person is in front of the camera.

**People already have a national identity document.**
They do, and you keep using it for your first check. 12iD does not replace it. It carries the result of your check forward, so the person does not present the document again, and it adds what the document cannot: proof of one fact without the rest, and proof of who is present.

**Why not a general wallet app that people install themselves?**
A general wallet belongs to someone else's brand, and people must find it, install it and learn it. 12iD puts the wallet inside the app your customers already use, and delivers the wallet, the face check and the console as one solution.

### 6.6 Risk and fraud

**Does 12iD stop all fraud?**
No method does. 12iD closes the routes that depend on a forged document, a stolen code, a copied credential or a borrowed device. A person who is tricked into approving a request themselves remains a risk. Showing them exactly what they are approving, inside your own app, reduces it.

**What evidence do we have after a disputed action?**
The record in the console shows what was asked, when, and that it was approved from the person's wallet. Where the face check applied, its result is part of the record.

**Can we change where the face check applies?**
Yes, yourselves, in the console. You set it for each credential and each kind of request. For a request, a rule can combine up to five conditions on numbers your systems send with it, such as an amount, a risk score or whether the device is new.

**Can a modified app skip the face check?**
No. 12iD enforces the check, not the app. The credential is not issued, and the result is not released to you, until the check has passed.
